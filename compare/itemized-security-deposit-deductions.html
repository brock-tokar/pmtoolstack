How to write an itemized security deposit deduction list

What to include in the statement you send a tenant after move out, with an example layout, timing rules to check in Canada and the US, and tools that build it for you.

What the statement is for

When you keep any part of a security or damage deposit, the tenant is usually entitled to know exactly why. An itemized deduction list, sometimes called a statement of account or a deposit disposition letter, shows the deposit held, each deduction with a reason and an amount, and the balance returned or owing.

A clear statement does two jobs. It meets your legal duty where one applies, and it heads off disputes because the tenant can see each charge and the photo behind it.

What to include

  • Tenant and property details. Tenant names, rental address, tenancy start and end dates, and the forwarding address you are sending it to.
  • Deposit held. The original deposit amount, plus any interest if your province or state requires it.
  • Each deduction on its own line. Room or area, a short description, whether it is damage, cleaning, unpaid rent or another allowed item, and the amount.
  • Evidence. A reference to the move in and move out photos for each line, and receipts or quotes where you have them.
  • Totals. Total deductions, and the balance returned to the tenant or still owing.
  • Date and signature. The date you sent it and who prepared it.

Timing and rules to check

Every province and state sets its own deadline and format. A few examples landlords often ask about:

  • Alberta. The landlord must return the deposit, or send a statement of account for any deductions, within 10 days after the tenant gives up possession.
  • British Columbia. Generally within 15 days after the later of the tenancy ending and receiving the tenant's forwarding address in writing, unless the tenant agrees in writing or the landlord applies for dispute resolution.
  • Ontario. Damage deposits are generally not allowed, so there is usually no damage deposit to deduct from. Damage claims go through the Landlord and Tenant Board.
  • California. An itemized statement and any refund are due within 21 days after the tenant moves out.
  • Texas. The refund and a written description of deductions are due within 30 days after the tenant surrenders the premises.
  • Florida. If the landlord intends to make a claim, written notice must go to the tenant by certified mail within 30 days of the tenant vacating, otherwise the deposit is returned within 15 days.

Rules change and have exceptions. This is general information, not legal advice. Confirm the current rules for your province or state before you send.

Questions

Do I have to give the tenant an itemized list of deductions?

In most provinces and states, yes, when you keep any part of the deposit. The format and deadline vary, so check your local rules.

How long do I have to send it?

It varies. For example, Alberta is 10 days after the tenant gives up possession, California is 21 days and Texas is 30 days. Confirm the current rule where your rental is.

Can I deduct for cleaning?

Usually only for cleaning beyond normal, such as heavy grease, debris or pet mess, and only if your local rules allow it. Record photos and the time or cost.

What if the tenant disputes the deductions?

Share the photos and receipts, try to agree in writing, and if that fails follow your local tenancy board or small claims process.
