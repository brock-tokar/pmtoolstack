Rental inspection checklist for move in and move out

A practical, room by room checklist for Canada and US landlords, plus the apps that turn it into a timestamped report.

Before you walk the unit

A good inspection starts before you open the door. Whether you rent in Canada or the US, the goal is the same: a dated, photo backed record of the unit that you and the tenant both have a copy of.

  • Book the walkthrough with the tenant and give written notice that meets your province or state rules.
  • Bring the lease, the keys list and, at move out, the signed move in report for the same unit.
  • Use one checklist for every unit so move in and move out notes line up item by item.
  • Charge your phone. Every photo should carry a date and time.

Move in checklist, room by room

Rate each item (good, fair, damaged) and add a short note plus a photo for anything that is not in good condition. The tenant should review and sign before they take possession.

Entry and hallways

  • Front door, locks, deadbolt and peephole
  • Walls, ceilings, trim and baseboards
  • Flooring, light fixtures and switches
  • Closets, shelves and doors

Kitchen

  • Fridge, stove, oven, range hood and dishwasher
  • Countertops, backsplash and cabinets inside and out
  • Sink, faucet, drain and under sink area
  • Microwave, garburator and outlets

Bathrooms

  • Toilet, tub, shower, grout and caulking
  • Vanity, sink, faucet, mirror and medicine cabinet
  • Exhaust fan, towel bars and toilet paper holder
  • Signs of leaks or water damage

Bedrooms and living areas

  • Walls, ceilings, paint and nail holes
  • Carpet or flooring, noting stains or wear
  • Windows, screens, blinds and window coverings
  • Closet doors, outlets and light fixtures

Exterior, garage and yard

  • Balcony, deck, patio and railings
  • Garage door, remotes and parking stall
  • Lawn, landscaping, fences and sheds
  • Garbage and recycling bins

Utilities, safety and keys

  • Meter readings for power, gas and water with a photo of each meter
  • Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms tested
  • Furnace filter, water heater and laundry machines
  • Every key, fob, mailbox key and remote counted and listed

Photo rules that hold up later

  • Take one wide shot of every wall in every room, then close ups of any existing damage.
  • Make sure every photo is timestamped and tied to the room and item it shows.
  • Shoot the same angles at move out so the before and after are easy to compare.
  • Photograph meters, keys and appliance interiors, not just walls and floors.
  • Store the finished report where you can send it to the tenant the same day.

Move out checklist

The move out inspection uses the same list, compared line by line against the move in report. This is the step that decides what happens with the damage deposit.

  • Walk the same rooms in the same order and note any change in condition since move in.
  • Separate normal wear and tear from damage, and take a photo of every damage item.
  • Check cleaning: appliances, floors, bathrooms, windows and inside cabinets.
  • Record final meter readings and collect every key, fob and remote on the keys list.
  • Get the tenant forwarding address for the deposit return or statement.
  • Itemize each repair or cleaning charge with a price, so the deposit math is clear before anyone leaves the unit.

Questions

What should a rental inspection checklist include?

Every room and its fixtures, appliances, flooring, walls, windows, exterior areas, meter readings, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and a list of every key, fob and remote, each with a condition rating, notes and timestamped photos.

Do I need a move in and move out inspection in Canada?

Rules vary by province. Some provinces, Alberta and British Columbia for example, expect written move in and move out inspection reports, and skipping them can weaken a landlord claim against the deposit. Check your provincial residential tenancy rules. This is not legal advice.

What about US landlords?

Rules vary by state. Many states require an itemized statement of deposit deductions within a set number of days after move out, so a dated move in and move out report with photos makes those statements much easier to support. Check your state rules.

Should the tenant sign the inspection report?

Yes. Have the tenant review and sign the move in report and give them a copy. At move out, invite them to attend the walkthrough and send them the finished report.
