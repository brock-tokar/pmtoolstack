How to settle security deposit disputes

Photo backed move out reports with itemized, priced repair charges settle damage deposits faster. Here is how, and which tools help.

Why deposit disputes happen

Most security deposit disputes are not about the damage itself. They are about proof. A tenant pushes back when the charges look vague, arrive late or cannot be tied to the condition of the unit at move in.

  • No signed move in report to compare against.
  • Photos with no date, or no photos at all.
  • Charges listed as one lump sum instead of item by item.
  • Normal wear and tear charged as damage.
  • A deposit statement sent after the deadline in your province or state.

What a defensible move out report looks like

  • The same room by room checklist used at move in, compared line by line. See our rental inspection checklist.
  • Timestamped photos of every damage item, shot from the same angles as the move in photos.
  • Final meter readings and a keys returned list.
  • Each repair or cleaning item listed separately with its own price.
  • A clear total showing the deposit returned and any amount charged back to the tenant.
  • A copy sent to the tenant promptly, ideally the same day.

Itemize and price charges on site

The fastest way to settle a damage deposit is to price each charge during the move out walkthrough, while you are standing in front of it, instead of building a spreadsheet days later. The tenant sees exactly what each charge is for and how the total was reached.

Normal wear and tear versus damage

Deductions should cover damage beyond normal use, not the unit simply getting older. Typical examples:

Usually normal wear and tear

  • Minor scuffs and faded paint
  • Light carpet wear in walkways
  • A few small nail holes from pictures
  • Loose door handles from regular use

Usually damage

  • Large holes or gouges in walls
  • Pet stains, burns or tears in carpet
  • Broken blinds, windows or appliance parts
  • Missing keys, fobs or remotes, and heavy cleaning left undone

Local rules and tribunals decide the final line, so check your province or state guidance. For room by room examples, see Normal wear and tear vs damage. Questions

How do I avoid a security deposit dispute?

Do a signed move in inspection with timestamped photos, repeat the same checklist at move out, itemize each charge with its own price, and send the tenant the report and deposit statement before your local deadline.

How long do I have to return a damage deposit?

It depends on your province or state. Deadlines are usually counted in days or weeks after the tenancy ends, so check your local residential tenancy rules and send the itemized statement before the deadline. This is not legal advice.

Can I charge a tenant for repairs after move out?

Generally you can deduct the reasonable cost of damage beyond normal wear and tear, backed by your move in and move out reports. Charges should be itemized and priced so the tenant can see how the total was reached.

What if the tenant disagrees with the charges?

Share the move in and move out reports side by side with the photos for each item. If you still cannot agree, your province or state tenancy board or small claims court will usually look at that same evidence.
